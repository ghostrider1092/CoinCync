Cynstra

Privacy in concentric layers — where no single break exposes the whole.

CoinCync testnet → Cynstra mainnet RandomX · CPU-only Mandatory privacy
Abstract

Abstract

Cynstra is a permissionless, mandatory-privacy cryptocurrency mined on commodity CPUs. Its central contribution is not a new cryptographic primitive — the primitives it uses are proven, and in several cases hardened over years by the work of others — but an architecture: Concentric Privacy.

Concentric Privacy is a defense-in-depth model in which independent privacy layers each guard a different attack surface, degrade independently, and are composed so that the failure of one layer does not deanonymize another. Consensus is fail-operational — the chain keeps producing valid blocks when a component fails; privacy is fail-closed — the protocol never silently downgrades to a weaker-privacy transaction. These normally-opposing postures coexist because the layered structure absorbs the failure of any single mechanism: no one break reaches the center.

01

Motivation

Financial privacy is not a feature; it is a precondition for economic freedom. A payment system that records who paid whom, how much, and when — in a permanent, public ledger — is a surveillance system, regardless of intent. The protocol cannot distinguish monitoring from surveillance: it does not know who is asking. It protects everyone equally, or it protects no one reliably.

Three failures motivate the design:

  • Optional privacy fails. When a chain offers both transparent and shielded transactions, the transparent set leaks metadata about the shielded set, and pressure pushes users toward the transparent default. Privacy that can be toggled is privacy that will be toggled off — usually by those who need it most.
  • Monolithic privacy fails catastrophically. Systems that stake their guarantees on a single mechanism have a single point of failure — from ring-signature traceability analyses to a zero-knowledge parameter bug that could have silently inflated an entire supply.
  • Proof-of-work centralizes. When mining is captured by specialized hardware and pools, "permissionless" erodes into a handful of gatekeepers.

Cynstra answers each: privacy is mandatory, privacy is layered (no single point of failure), and mining is CPU-only via RandomX so participation stays broad. This is built with deep respect for the projects that came before — Monero above all, which proved over nearly a decade, under real regulatory and academic pressure, that mandatory privacy can survive at scale. Cynstra does not aim to replace that work; it explores the layers those projects deprioritized and contributes what it learns back to the privacy commons.

02

Privacy is a movement, not a market

The adversary is not another privacy coin. It is financial surveillance. Against that adversary, every serious privacy project is on the same side.

This reframing has a concrete consequence for honesty. Because Cynstra is not trying to beat anyone, it is under no pressure to overclaim. Where it reuses proven mechanisms, it says so. Where its own additions are younger and less battle-tested than the mechanisms they sit beside, it says that too. A guarantee that cannot survive adversarial scrutiny is not a guarantee; it is marketing. This paper is written to invite that scrutiny, not to deflect it.

There is also a resilience argument — the same principle this paper is built on, one level up. A privacy ecosystem with many independent implementations, built differently and failing differently, is harder to attack, regulate, or extinguish than any single chain. Diversity is defense in depth for the movement itself.

03

Concentric Privacy: the architecture

A transaction leaks along several independent axes: who sent it, who received it, how much, whether it links to other transactions, where on the network it originated, and whether any of it ties to a real human. Concentric Privacy assigns each axis to a distinct layer and arranges the layers as concentric rings around the thing that matters most.

The center: what we are protecting

For a privacy currency, the catastrophic, irreversible exposure is not the amount or the network origin — those leak badly but are survivable. It is the link between a human identity and their on-chain activity. On a public, immutable ledger, once made, that link is made forever. So the center of the design — the keep — is not "the transaction." It is the person. Two principles govern the geometry: the riskiest asset sits at the center behind the most layers; and the innermost wall — the last line of defense — must be the strongest, most-proven mechanism, not the newest.

The rings (as an attacker meets them)

  • Ring 4 · Network. Where a transaction entered the P2P network, from what address, at what instant — guarded by Dandelion++ and traffic shaping (timing jitter and cover packets today; size normalization is specified but not yet wired into the send path). Break it, and you learn a transaction exists — not what it says.
  • Ring 3 · Linkability. Can outputs be connected, a wallet clustered, value followed over time? Guarded by decoy defense, key images, view tags, and automatic churn. Break it, and you may cluster activity — not identity or amount.
  • Ring 2 · Transaction content. Sender, receiver, amount — guarded by CLSAG ring-16, stealth addresses, and Bulletproofs+ commitments. The ring monolithic designs stake everything on; alone, one crack is fatal.
  • Ring 1 · The user. Even with all the above intact — can the person be coerced or their wallet exploited? Live today: encrypted memos and watch-only view keys. Specified and in progress: scoped view keys, deniable wallets (creation currently disabled pending a structural rewrite), and a dead-man's switch (recovery metadata is carried on-chain; consensus enforcement not yet wired). The ring almost no chain even attempts — marked here honestly as design ahead of implementation.

The arrangement is not decoration. Its purpose is graceful degradation: each ring stands on its own, so the failure of any one leaves the others holding. The layers must be connected in coverage (each guards a different door) but independent in failure (none is load-bearing on another). A lattice whose walls hold each other up collapses in a chain reaction; concentric, independent rings do not.

04

Fault isolation and the two-posture rule

Concentric Privacy borrows deliberately from reliability engineering — the bulkhead (compartmentalize so a breach cannot flood the ship), graceful degradation, the circuit breaker, and redundancy. But privacy inverts one assumption these patterns take for granted, and the inversion is the crux.

In availability engineering, failure is recoverable — the service returns, no lasting harm. In privacy, failure is permanent: a deanonymization committed to an immutable ledger cannot be undone. This forces a split ordinary systems never make:

  • Consensus and availability are fail-operational. A broken privacy feature must never halt the chain; blocks keep flowing on the remaining good parts.
  • Privacy is fail-closed. The protocol must never quietly "keep running at reduced privacy," because reduced privacy means active, permanent leakage. The charter encodes this: no transparent mode, no reduced-privacy transaction class.

Ordinarily these fight — fail-closed says halt if you cannot guarantee privacy; fail-operational says keep going. The concentric structure resolves the conflict. Because the other rings still hold when one fails, a single break never forces the choice between halting the chain and shipping weak privacy. The structure absorbs the failure: consensus continues, privacy stays whole minus one layer of margin, and the broken ring is isolated and patched. Defense-in-depth is precisely the mechanism that makes fail-operational-on-consensus and fail-closed-on-privacy achievable at the same time — something a monolith cannot do.

Honest cost

Isolating a broken privacy layer is not free the way restarting a failed web service is. The remaining rings carry the load, but the margin is thinner, and transactions made while a layer is disabled are permanently less protected. Defense-in-depth cushions this; it does not erase it.

05

Composition safety

Stacking privacy features does not compose for free. Each mechanism hides one thing, but in doing so can create a new observable regularity — a distinguisher — that an adversary uses to undo a different mechanism. More privacy features can mean less privacy if the interactions are wrong. This is the discipline most often skipped, and where privacy quietly dies.

  • Automatic churn vs. timing patterns. Churn on a fixed rhythm or in round amounts becomes a fingerprint that re-links the outputs it was meant to unlink. It must be randomized in timing and amount so there is no cadence to lock onto.
  • Traffic shaping vs. Dandelion++. Both are timing-based defenses on the same packets. Shaping that perturbs Dandelion's stem timing, or cover traffic distinguishable from real relay traffic, weakens the origin protection. The layers must coordinate, not depend: enough to avoid collision, never enough to make one load-bearing on the other.
  • Padding vs. message framing. Size-normalization padding must not be parseable as another message type. In development this surfaced as a concrete framing conflict, resolved by giving padding a distinct, first-class message type rather than a hack — an instance of the discipline, not a footnote to it.

The claim is therefore precise: not "many privacy features," but "many privacy features that do not eat each other." Demonstrating the second is the harder, more valuable half, treated here as a first-class obligation.

Honest limitation

Breadth is not strength by itself. Every added mechanism is a potential new distinguisher, and Cynstra's stack is broader — more interaction surface — than the deliberately minimal designs it admires, and its layers are younger and less scrutinized. The analysis here is a beginning, not a proof; a systematic argument that no cross-layer distinguisher exists is future work, stated openly and offered for scrutiny.

06

Consensus, economics & parameters

Every value below is a named constant in the reference implementation (src/constants.rs unless noted), cited so it can be checked against the code. No figure here is approximated.

Proof of work

  • Algorithm: RandomX — CPU-optimized, ASIC-resistant. Full-memory (~2 GB dataset) or light (256 MB cache); the key rotates every 2,048 blocks (RANDOMX_KEY_EPOCH). A laptop competes with a server.
  • Target block time: 120 s (TARGET_BLOCK_TIME).
  • Difficulty: ASERT, retargeting every block with a 1-hour halflife (ASERT_HALFLIFE = 3600), short/long windows (8/144) blended 70/30. Per-block retargeting absorbs hashrate swings smoothly rather than in epochs.

Supply & emission

Maximum supply 100,000,000 CYNC (MAX_SUPPLY), 12 decimals. The curve is smooth and halving-free — each block pays max(TAIL_EMISSION, (100,000,000 − mined) × COIN / 2,000,000), proportional to the remaining supply:

A 0.6 CYNC per-block tail emission (TAIL_EMISSION) is perpetual — a permanent, predictable security subsidy so miners are still paid as the cap is approached.

Fees & the burn

  • No premine, no dev tax, no protocol fee — Article II, enforced as FEE_PROTOCOL_NORMAL_PERCENT = 0.
  • 70% to the miner, 30% burned (FEE_MINER_NORMAL_PERCENT / FEE_BURN_NORMAL_PERCENT). Under congestion the miner share falls to 50% and the burn rises — spam gets more expensive exactly when the network is contended. The burn is a protocol invariant, not a tunable knob.

Privacy parameters

  • Ring size 16 (RING_SIZE) from block 10,000 onward; an 11-member bootstrap floor (BOOTSTRAP_MIN_RING_SIZE) applies earlier when a young chain lacks outputs to form a full ring. Ceiling 32; an increase above 16 is specified in CIP-017.
  • Pedersen commitments with Bulletproofs+ range proofs; one-time stealth addresses; 1-byte view tags.
07

Governance: constitutional consensus

Most cryptocurrency governance is social — a promise, a foundation's goodwill, a multisig's restraint. Cynstra's founding guarantees are mechanical: enforced by the build system and the compiler, so violating them is not a policy dispute but a build failure.

The project ships a Constitution (nineteen Articles) and a Bill of Rights (fifteen Rights) as protocol documents — Fixed Supply (I), No Pre-mine / No Dev Tax (II), Mandatory Privacy (III), Open Mining (V), No Surveillance Infrastructure (IX), and more.

Two mechanical enforcement layers

Hash-locked consensus files. Eight files are pinned by SHA-256 in critical_files.lock:

CONSTITUTION.md        src/consensus/difficulty.rs
docs/BILL_OF_RIGHTS.md src/consensus/pow.rs
src/testnet.rs         src/consensus/validation.rs
src/constants.rs       src/emission/curve.rs

build.rs recomputes each hash (line endings normalized) at the start of every build; if any drifts, the build fails. Changing one requires an explicit, reviewed refresh — the emission curve, the validation rules, and the Constitution itself cannot be altered silently.

Compile-time tripwires. The substantive invariants are also encoded as const assertions the compiler evaluates:

const _: () = assert!(DEV_TAX_PERCENT == 0, …);        // Article II
const _: () = assert!(MANDATORY_CONFIDENTIAL, …);      // Article III
const _: () = assert!(RANDOMX_ONLY, …);                // Article V
const _: () = assert!(!ADDRESS_BLACKLIST_ENABLED, …);  // Article IX
const _: () = assert!(!SURVEILLANCE_HOOKS_ENABLED, …);
const _: () = assert!(NO_ADMIN_KEYS, …);
const _: () = assert!(NO_ALGORITHMIC_PEG, …);          // the Terra failure mode

These are not runtime checks a flag can disable — they are evaluated when the code is compiled. A developer who tries to add a dev tax, a blacklist, an admin key, or an algorithmic peg does not ship a controversial change; their code does not compile. The guarantee is not "we promise not to" — it is "the software cannot be built to do this."

Honest limit

A fork can always strip these guardrails — delete the asserts, re-lock the hashes. What the mechanism prevents is silent or accidental subversion, and it makes deliberate subversion loud: a fork that removes the Constitution is visibly a different chain, not a stealth upgrade of this one. The guarantee is not that the rules can never change — it is that they cannot change invisibly.

Comparison

This positions Cynstra by contribution, not superiority. On transaction and linkability it uses proven mechanisms comparable to Monero's — no claim to beat them there. Its distinctive coverage is network, operational, mandatory, and constitutional.

  • Transaction & linkability: Cynstra matches Monero (CLSAG, stealth, Bulletproofs+); Zcash's zk-SNARK shielding is arguably stronger. No superiority claim here.
  • Network: most stop at Dandelion++ (Monero's Kovri/I2P was shelved ~2019). Cynstra adds integrated traffic shaping — the one row where it is broader than the field.
  • Operational: auto-churn and encrypted memos ship today; the deniable-wallet / dead-man's-switch / scoped-view-key layer is specified and, to our knowledge, unique among shipping chains in even being attempted — marked as design ahead of implementation, not claimed as delivered.
The caveat that belongs with the table

A ✓ means the mechanism is present and active — not that it has survived a decade of adversarial attack. Monero's ✓s are battle-tested; several of Cynstra's — especially the network and operational rows that make it distinctive — are younger and far less scrutinized. Breadth of coverage is a real, claimable property. Proven strength is earned over years of people trying to break you, and Cynstra has not yet earned it. This paper claims the former and explicitly disclaims the latter.

09

Conclusion

Cynstra's thesis is a single sentence: privacy in concentric layers, where no single break exposes the whole. It does not rest its guarantees on one clever mechanism; it layers independent defenses so a failure in any one degrades the margin without collapsing the whole, keeps consensus alive while refusing to ship weakened privacy, and takes seriously the discipline of keeping those layers from interfering. It is offered not as a competitor to the projects that proved this fight can be won, but as a fellow traveler exploring a corner they left open — and as one more independent line of defense for a cause that is safer for having many.
